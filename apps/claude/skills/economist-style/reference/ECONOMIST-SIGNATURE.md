# Economist Signature Rules

The compact rules that make copy recognizably Economist. Apply these first; load the detailed reference files only when a deeper check is needed.

**New Zealand overrides.** Rules marked (NZ) below replace the Economist's own on dates,
currency, dashes and honorifics. Rules marked (B) are British-dialect rules that NZ English also
follows. See `DIALECT-CONVENTIONS.md` for the full NZ section, including te reo Māori.

## Numbers
- Spell out one to ten; figures from 11
- Money: $5m, $3.2b, $1t (symbol before, no space, abbreviated). (NZ) A bare $ is New Zealand dollars; prefix every other dollar (US$, A$)
- Percentages: % with figures ("5%"); "percentage points" for changes in rates
- Never start a sentence with a figure — spell it out or rewrite

## Dates and times
- (NZ) Day first, no ordinal, no comma: "5 April", "1 January 2025". The Economist's own "April 5th" form applies only to documents already using it
- Decades without apostrophe: "the 1990s"
- Times: "3pm", "9.30am"; prefer "noon" and "midnight"

## Punctuation
- No serial (Oxford) comma unless needed for clarity: "tax, spending and regulation"
- Double quotation marks, single inside; punctuation outside quotes unless part of the original
- No full stops in abbreviations: Mr, Dr, GDP, eg, ie (a comma follows "ie")
- Initials in names take points: J.K. Rowling
- Hyphenate co-operate, co-ordinate, pre-war, post-war
- (NZ) Dashes: spaced en dash ( – ) to set off a clause; unspaced en dash for ranges (1–3 June); no em dashes, and never a hyphen as a dash
- No exclamation marks; rhetorical questions rarely (relaxed for first-person essays and posts: see Register in `SKILL.md`)

## People
- (NZ) Full name on first mention; surname alone thereafter. NZ practice drops the Economist's honorific pattern ("Janet Yellen... Yellen", not "Ms Yellen")
- Job titles lower case: "Jerome Powell, chairman of the Federal Reserve" — never "Chairman Powell"
- "Chairman" is retained; the Economist dislikes "chair" and "chairperson" for people

## Grammar
- Companies, governments and parties are singular: "the government is"
- "Will", not "shall"
- Short words, active voice, one idea per sentence

## Headings
- Sentence case only: "The rise and fall of economic theory"

## Spelling (B)
- -ise endings: organise, realise
- "focused"
- (NZ) "judgement", not the Economist's "judgment"
